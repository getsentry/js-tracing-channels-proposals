## 📊 Ecosystem Coverage

How much of the Node ecosystem can be natively traced today, and how much of that is Sentry's doing. **Adoption-aware**, so it doesn't inflate freshly-merged work.

> **Method** (`scripts/coverage.py`, re-run monthly):
> - **Denominator** = the whole tracked ecosystem, **all versions** (weekly npm downloads). Old un-upgraded installs stay in — they genuinely aren't traced.
> - **Numerator** = only downloads on **channel-capable versions** (the release that introduced the channel, and later). This measures *real adoption*, not "the channel exists somewhere" — so `graphql` (channel only in v17.0.0-rc.0) and a days-old `mongoose` v9.7.0 correctly contribute ~0 until users upgrade.
> - **Two numbers + diff:** coverage *without* Sentry (the baseline the ecosystem reached on its own, dominated by undici/Node-core), coverage *with* Sentry's merges, and the difference = Sentry's measurable effect.
> - **Caveat that remains:** downloads ≠ distinct apps (libraries are co-used; transitive/CI pulls inflate counts). Read these as download-weighted coverage, not "% of applications."

**Fair single statement:** Sentry has merged native tracing into libraries representing **~14% of the ecosystem's weekly downloads** (the ceiling at full adoption); **~1.6 points is adopted in production today**, with the rest landing as users upgrade.

Why Sentry's adopted-today number is modest: the channels we merged are recent, and adoption lags merges by months. The **ceiling** (downloads of Sentry-touched libraries at full upgrade) is the payoff still in flight. Independent work — chiefly **undici (~476M/mo, Node core)** — dominates the baseline and is not Sentry's to claim.
